\section{Model}

\subsection{Objective}

\[
\min_{y,q,O,U}\quad
\underbrace{\sum_{c} F_c y_c}_{\text{entry costs}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{c} K_c q_c}_{\text{vehicle costs}}
+\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{c\in C}\Big[
\underbrace{c_c^{+}O_c^{s}}_{\text{unused capacity}}
+\underbrace{c_c^{-}U_c^{s}}_{\text{foregone margin}}
\Big].
\]

The superscripts in $c_c^{+}$ and $c_c^{-}$ denote \emph{excess} and \emph{shortage},
not the sign of the cost. Scenarios are weighted equally ($1/|S|$).

\subsection{Constraints}

\[
\begin{aligned}
&0\le q_c\le \bar q_c\, y_c && \forall c\in C,\\
&\textstyle\sum_{c} q_c\le Q, &&\\
&\kappa_c q_c - M_c p_c^{s} = O_c^{s}-U_c^{s} && \forall c\in C,\ s\in S,\\
&y_c\in\{0,1\},\quad q_c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, &&\\
&O_c^{s},\,U_c^{s}\ge 0. &&
\end{aligned}
\]

{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.32\linewidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
Constraint & Meaning \\ \midrule
$0\le q_c\le\bar q_c y_c$ & No vehicles in a city that is not entered; up to the city-specific limit if it is entered. \\
$\sum_c q_c\le Q$ & Total fleet limit. \\
$\kappa_c q_c-M_c p_c^s=O_c^s-U_c^s$ & Service capacity minus robotaxi demand equals unused capacity minus unmet demand. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
}

\section{Numerical experiments with synthetic data (summary)}

Two synthetic instances were run to answer two questions: (1) is the implementation correct and does it
produce meaningful results, and (2) how sensitive are the results to parameter choices.
Full details are in \texttt{numerical\_experiments.tex} (small instance) and \texttt{annual\_experiments.tex} (annual instance).

\subsection{Small case instance (3 cities [A,B,C], $Q=4$, 10 hand-designed scenarios)}

\textbf{Inputs.} $M=(80,100,120)$, $\kappa=10$, $\bar q=2$, $F=20$, $K=5$, $c^+=0$, $c^-=10$; mean choice probability 0.38 in every city.

\textbf{Correctness.} The MILP (SciPy/HiGHS) and full enumeration of all 60 feasible $(y,q)$ give the same unique optimum $q=(0,2,2)$, $Z=858$.
Eight edge cases behave as expected: no vehicles, no demand, $c^-=0$, or a prohibitive $F$ give no entry; $Q=8$ stops at the city limits $(2,2,2)$;
$F=0$ enters all three cities; demand equal to one vehicle's capacity gives $(1,1,1)$ with $U=O=0$.

\textbf{Meaningful results.} The model concentrates in the two larger markets: entering A would serve 0.8 more trips (margin 8) but costs $F=20$, so $Z$ rises by 12.
Service rate 30\%, utilization 85.5\%, improvement over no entry $\Delta Z=282$.

\textbf{Sensitivity.} Switching points are identified rather than only three levels:
three cities for $F<8$, B and C for $8<F<160$, C alone for $160<F<162$, no entry above;
three cities from $\kappa\ge12.5$ (tie at 12); $(2,0,2)$ from $M_A\ge1.3\times$ (tie at 1.25);
$c^+$ leaves the allocation unchanged up to 15 and switches to $(1,1,2)$ from 15.5;
$Q\ge6$ is capped by the city limits. Without demand variability three allocations tie; with it only $(0,2,2)$ is optimal,
and mean-demand allocations cost 0--16 more under the scenarios.

\subsection{Large case instance (3 cities [A,B,C], $Q=1{,}000$, 10 random scenarios)}

\textbf{Inputs.} Literature-based proxies: $M=(577.9, 770.5, 963.2)$ million trips/year, $\kappa=8{,}760$, $\bar q=500$, $F=3.5$M, $K=7{,}972$, $c^+=0$, $c^-=10$;
$p_c^s$ drawn independently from $\{0.002,0.005,0.01,0.02\}$ so that demand and capacity are of the same order.

\textbf{Correctness.} Every solve was cross-checked against an independent dynamic program that also counts tied optima (the program itself was checked against enumeration on the small instance); all edge cases pass.

\textbf{Meaningful results.} Optimum $q=(132,368,500)$ with 72 ties in the B--C split; $\Delta Z=60.85$M, service rate 34.9\%, utilization 90.6\%.
The allocation follows the value of one more vehicle, $87{,}600\times P(\text{demand}>\text{capacity})-7{,}972$: A is filled only while all ten scenarios have unmet demand.
Entering A is worth only 0.39M more than $(0,500,500)$.
With the reference choice probabilities ($\ge0.02$) demand exceeds capacity in every scenario and any two cities tie regardless of $M$ and $p$; demand differences matter only near $p\approx\kappa\bar q/M$.

\textbf{Sensitivity.} A drops out when $F>3.89$M, $\kappa\le8{,}212.5$, or demand grows by $\ge6.25\%$; A enters only for $Q\ge1{,}000$ and the city limits bind at 1{,}500;
$\bar q\ge750$ gives $(0,440,560)$; $c^-=2,5,10$ give one, two, three cities.
$c^+$ changes nothing up to 15 (it only lowers $\Delta Z$ by $827{,}397\,c^+$) and shrinks the fleet to 528 at 40; it is equivalent to raising $K$ by $c^+\kappa$ and $c^-$ by $c^+$.
Mean-demand allocations cost 0.39--8.14M more under the scenarios, and 50-scenario samples give seed-dependent allocations while 500-scenario samples are stable.
