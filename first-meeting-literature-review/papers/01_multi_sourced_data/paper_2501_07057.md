# Optimization with Multi-sourced Reference Information and Unknown Trust: A Distributionally Robust Approach

## One-Sentence Summary

This paper develops a multi-reference distributionally robust optimization framework where several information sources provide predictions for the same uncertain parameter, but their reliability is unknown and updated over time based on realized prediction errors.

## 1. Main Problem

The paper studies decision-making under uncertainty when there are multiple sources of information.

In classical stochastic optimization, the decision-maker often assumes that the true distribution of uncertainty is known. In standard DRO, the decision-maker usually builds an ambiguity set around one empirical distribution.

However, in many real-world problems, there are several information sources:

- sensors;
- official reports;
- social media;
- weather forecasts;
- simulation outputs;
- machine learning models;
- expert forecasts.

These sources may provide different predictions for the same uncertain parameter, and their reliability may not be known in advance.

The key question is:

> How should we make robust decisions when multiple sources provide imperfect and possibly conflicting predictions, and we do not know how much to trust each source?

## 2. Core Idea

The paper combines three ideas:

1. **Multi-source prediction**
   - Each source provides a prediction for the same uncertain parameter.

2. **Trust update**
   - Each source receives a trust weight based on its historical prediction errors.

3. **Wasserstein DRO**
   - The trust-weighted empirical distribution is used as the center of a Wasserstein ambiguity set.
   - The decision is chosen to minimize worst-case expected loss over this ambiguity set.
