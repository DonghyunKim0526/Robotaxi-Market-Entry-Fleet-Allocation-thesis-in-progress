# Candidate driving factors

## Candidate variables and their model roles

| Component | Candidate families | Measurement examples |
|---|---|---|
| Market scale $M$ | Population, jobs/economic activity, income, car availability, land use, airport/visitor activity | ACS, LEHD/LODES, EPA SLD, FAA |
| Choice probability $p$ | Relative fare/wait/time, zero-vehicle share, age, experience, service features, attitudes | ACS, matched service records, choice experiments, common surveys |
| Feasibility / availability | Regulatory permission, ODD, weather-related service limits | Official operating conditions and public environmental data |

## How to interpret the evidence

- Papers on conventional ride-hailing describe realized usage; they do not directly observe the unrealized opportunities included in $M$.
- Robotaxi trip-count associations can combine scale and choice effects. Do not treat every candidate as an identified coefficient of $p$.
- Own fare, waiting time, and features may be operator-controlled; city demographics are exogenous candidates. A decision-dependent extension needs data linking changes in service to choices.
- Time/weather effects and city characteristics play different roles in a one-year entry model. Shared geography, year, and denominator are required before pooling observations.

The list is a screening pool, not a fitted predictor or the final selection. The subsequent selection documents fix $M$ using trip generation and prioritize zero-vehicle share and age composition for exogenous $p$ inputs.
