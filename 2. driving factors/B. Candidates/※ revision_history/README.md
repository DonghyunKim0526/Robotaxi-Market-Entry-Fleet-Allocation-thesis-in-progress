# Candidate-factor revision history

The 2026-10-01 archive contains both a greyed earlier choice-factor table and a revised expanded table. The current candidate document retains the revised table. This log separates that within-document revision from later editorial cleanup.

## Revision recorded within the 2026-10-01 archive

| Part changed | Earlier table → revised table | Reason / consequence supported by the revised descriptions |
|---|---|---|
| Scope of choice candidates | A short service/attitude list → expanded candidate pool including urban/socioeconomic characteristics and separate operating conditions | Makes candidate-city measurement and the role of each factor explicit; the candidate pool is still not a final fitted variable set |
| Fare and waiting time | General choice attributes → own fare as an operator decision and own waiting time as fleet/allocation dependent | Distinguishes exogenous city information from effects requiring a decision-dependent extension |
| Travel time and support/reviews | General attributes → distinguish operating-policy effects from traffic, and controllable features from past-use outcomes | Avoids assigning one exogenous role to conceptually mixed variables |
| Trust, price value, and habits | Broad combined evidence → explicitly notes that Wei et al. do not include trust and survey scores are not choice probabilities | Corrects the scope of the citation and the interpretation of the measured response |
| Age, knowledge, and experience | Broad individual differences → no uniform age sign; distinguish AV knowledge from service awareness; no uniform positive experience effect established | Avoids stronger directional or transfer claims than the cited evidence supports |
| Measurement requirements | General service/attitude measurements → linked choices, competing-service conditions, city measures, and operational records | Makes the data needed for each proposed role clearer; availability and comparability still require checking |

The reasons above summarize distinctions explicitly discussed in the revised table; they do not establish that the full candidate pool has been empirically validated.
